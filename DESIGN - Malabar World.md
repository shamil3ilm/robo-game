# Robo Run v.02 — Malabar World (design draft)

Working file: `Robo Run v.02.html` (copy of v.01; v.01 stays untouched).

## 1. Pitch
Robo is a delivery robot on a **food trail through Malabar**. Collect ingredients while running through real, recognisable places in Kozhikode, Malappuram and Wayanad, carry them in a backpack, cook dishes at a stall, and use the dishes as buffs for the next run.

Food is the reason to go everywhere, the reason to collect, and the progression system.

## 2. Principles
- **Keep the runner.** The 3-lane runner is the core; the open world is a hub around it.
- **Additive, not a rewrite.** The six existing zones stay as a "Classic" world. Malabar is a second world the player picks.
- **Recognisable, not photoreal.** Atmosphere (light, fog, water, sky), instanced vegetation and simple landmark silhouettes. Phones are a hard requirement, so there are quality tiers (the engine already has auto quality).
- **Respectful representation.** No pork or alcohol anywhere in food content. Wayanad's adivasi communities and food traditions are shown only with care. The 2024 Wayanad landslide is not used as a hazard or theme.

## 3a. Real-world setup: the rules every addition follows
**Rules.** (1) Something appears only where it really lives. (2) Each zone has its own light and weather: beach = golden evening in the fair season, Wayanad = cool misty morning, Malappuram (future) = bright afternoon over green paddy; monsoon is a weather event, not a zone. (3) Paths match the place. (4) Hazards are natural ones (potholes, mud, webs, fog); animals are never cruel targets. (5) Herbs and species are real and local. (6) Everything has a Low-quality fallback.

| | **Kozhikode coast** | **Wayanad ghats** | **Malappuram (future)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Terrain** | Flat laterite-backed coast, sand, red laterite rocks, old piers, sea | Western Ghats plateau, hairpin ghat road, red laterite cuts, valleys, estates | River and canal banks, paddy, teak plantation (Nilambur), betel-leaf farms (Tirur) |

## 4. Food, backpack, cooking
**Collectibles** (replace/extend energy and coins on the Malabar world):
- Ingredients per region (e.g. Wayanad: pepper, cardamom, coffee beans, Gandhakasala rice; Malappuram: betel leaf, mussels, coconut; Kozhikode: ghee, sugar, rice, tea).
- Rare relics (e.g. a boat-builder's tool, an old lamp) for a collection book.

**Backpack**
- Slot grid, upgraded with coins.
- Weight: a heavy pack slightly lowers speed or jump. This makes "what do I carry?" a real choice.
- Categories: ingredients, relics, consumables, tool parts.

**Cooking**
- At a stall in the hub, combine ingredients into a dish from a recipe book.
- Dishes give a buff for the next run: biryani = shield, halwa = score multiplier, sulaimani = magnet, kappa = extra health. Exact numbers go in a tuning object like `COMBAT`.
- A recipe book page per region, with silhouettes for undiscovered dishes. This feeds the badge system.

**Rivals** (reuse the combat system, re-themed): rival delivery bots and stall-rush bots. Boss at each zone end: a giant bot with a regional look.

## 5. Modes
- **Classic** (existing six zones).
- **Malabar Trail** (story: a vendor gives orders such as "bring ingredients for biryani").
- **Free Roam** (hub exploration, collecting, photos; no fail).
- **Daily Route** (existing daily challenge, seeded per day).
- **Kids / Explorer** (extends assist mode).
- **Football (Malappuram)**: the sevens-football craze as a weapon: kick a football instead of the pipe/wrench.

## 6. Tools
Unlocked per region, some collectibles reachable only with a tool:
- **Umbrella** (monsoon rain, glide): first tool, cheap to build.
- **Torch** (extends the cave headlight).
- **Grappling hook** (gaps, Wayanad cliffs).
- **Paddle** (Ponnani river and backwater sections).
- **Basket** (catches falling items).

## 7. Open-world hub (3D, third person)
Small, walkable areas, not one big map. Phone-friendly: short draw distance, instanced props, quality tiers.
- **Kozhikode hub:** Mittai Theruvu lane (sweet stalls, lamps) is the first one. It is narrow, cheap to render, and very recognisable.
- Gates in each hub lead to runner stages.
- Hub holds the cooking stall, backpack chest and recipe book.

## 8c. The city world: districts, the big wishlist, and Malappuram (plan; checked facts only)

## 8d. Money, billing, barter, a home for the robot, and EV charging (plan; the hit reactions are built)
**Built now: things react when you hit them (town).** A knock only counts if it is real (a graze or a slide along a wall does nothing, and one bump fires once, not for as long as you push). Then each thing answers in its own way: **people** stop, turn round and say sorry ("Sorry!", "Excuse me!", "Aiyyo!", ക്ഷമിക്കണം!, അയ്യോ!, ശ്രദ്ധിച്ച്!); **autos, scooters and buses** rock and honk ("Peep peep!" with a two-tone horn); **stalls** rattle ("Careful!"); **lamp posts** ring; **palms** shake and, at most once every two minutes per tree, drop a coconut that you can pick up (an ingredient); **walls** give a thud. Everything also gives a dust puff, a short camera shake, a flinch of the walker, and its own sound. Checked in the browser for a parked auto and a palm; people, stalls and poles use the same code path but have not been looked at one by one. The runner's own block-hit reactions (shattering, debris, freeze-frame, tumble) were done earlier.

**Money, as a real wallet.** Today everything is one pile of "coins". The plan: a **wallet** that holds three things, each with its own rules.
- **Cash (₹):** works everywhere, including tea stalls and autos; it is what you pick up.
- **GPay / UPI:** an account balance you top up at a bank or recharge counter; accepted wherever there is a QR code (shops, hotels, the mall, EV chargers), not at every street stall; every payment shows a transaction id and "Paid to <shop>".
- **Barter:** goods for goods, see below.
Other cards, loans and so on are out of scope.

**Billing, properly.** Every purchase becomes an itemised **bill**: bill number, date, shop, lines (item, quantity, rate, amount), total ("prices include tax"), how it was paid (cash, UPI with its transaction id, barter credits), and change. Shops get a small **cart** (add several things, then pay once). The last 30 bills are kept in a **Bills** list. Logic rules: prices come from one table; shops have limited stock and restock; unaffordable or unavailable items are greyed out with the reason.

**Barter, logically.** Every ingredient and scrap item has a fixed value in rupees (the supermarket price). A shop only accepts what it would actually use: the tea stall takes banana, jaggery, rice, coconut, tea and cardamom; the Vaidyasala takes pepper, cardamom, spice mix and jaggery; the hotels take rice, ghee, spice mix, coconut and banana; Swamy & Sons takes coffee, cardamom, tea and jaggery; hardware and textile shops take cash or UPI only. A shop credits **80%** of the value of what it uses, the supermarket and scrap dealer credit **60%** of everything they buy (no profit from buying and selling back). Barter credit and cash can be mixed on one bill; the bill shows both.

**Shoes, clothes and scrap.** *Shoes:* a footwear shop (chappals, sports shoes, football boots, gumboots) with real effects (walking speed, mud, grip on the runner). *Clothes:* the textile shop already sells characters; town clothes (mundu, shirt, saree, jersey) come next. *Scrap:* paper bundles, plastic bottles, tin cans and glass lie around town; a **scrap dealer** buys them by weight at a posted rate, as the neighbourhood *kabadi* does, and Kerala's Haritha Karma Sena collects plastic waste, so the idea is true to the place. Scrap is the poor man's income and feeds barter and cash.

**A home for the robot.** A small **dock** you can walk into: a charging cradle, a wardrobe (opens Characters), a stove (opens the Kitchen), a bed to rest, and a shelf for the backpack. It is the first room built with the new walk-in rooms system (the same one the hotels, the Zil Money office and the hospital use).

**Battery and EV charging.** The robot has a **battery**: walking and running use it slowly, an empty battery slows you down, a full one gives a small boost at the start of a run. Charging is free at home. Out in town it costs money at **KSEB EV charging stations** (KSEB is installing public chargers, including in Kozhikode and Malappuram) and bills by the unit with the real **time-of-day tariff** as reported in May 2025: AC (slow) ₹8.50 per unit from 9 am to 4 pm and ₹14.23 from 4 pm to 9 am; DC (fast) ₹16.50 by day and ₹23.23 from 4 pm to 9 am. Electric autos and KSRTC electric buses (KSRTC runs some) charge at the stands. These tariffs are quoted as reported; check the current rates before anyone relies on them.

## 8e. The web build, hosting, real-time world building and a Mini Militia mode (decisions and plan)

**What the game is as a build.** An HTML game: three.js in the browser, plain script files (no bundler, no framework to install), 60 files and about 2.8 MB in `src/`. **Decided: it ships as a website link** (a hosted folder that can be installed on a phone's home screen), not as a file you send and not (yet) as an app-store app. Built and tested: `node tools/build.cjs` makes `dist/` (a plain static folder, version-stamped by a hash of every file, nothing minified); `node tools/serve.cjs` serves `src/` for development and `node tools/serve.cjs --dist` serves the build as a real host would (add `--host 0.0.0.0` to open it on a phone over the same Wi-Fi); `src/manifest.webmanifest` and new robot icons in `src/img/` make it installable; `src/sw.js` keeps the whole game on the device so it opens with no signal and updates itself (a new publish is picked up on the next visit: the first visit after it still shows the old page, the next shows the new one). The worker registers only on a real https host, or on localhost when the address has `?sw`, so local work never serves stale code. Tried in the browser: installed worker, all 60 files cached, an update replacing the old cache, and a reload with the network switched off. `tests/build.test.js` checks that every file the page points at exists (the missing manifest and favicon were console errors for every visitor before this), the manifest, the worker, the version, the exclusion of backups, and the server's headers. **To publish:** upload the contents of `dist/` to any static host (Netlify, Cloudflare Pages, GitHub Pages) over https; send `index.html`, `sw.js` and the manifest with no-cache (the dev server shows the headers). **I did not publish anything:** that needs your hosting account. Notes: `Robo Run v.02.html` in the project folder is an old export from 24 October that has none of the later work, so treat `src/` and `dist/` as the game. The `apple-touch-icon.png` is still the original "RR" placeholder (I restored it byte for byte after overwriting it by mistake); the new robot icons are used for the install manifest and the page icon, and switching the iPhone icon too is a one-line change if you want it. The parent `Robo` folder also holds a separate project ("Monsoon Trails", with its own build scripts) that this work does not touch.

**Real-time world building: all four kinds are wanted, so the foundation is one idea, the world as a list of changes.** Today the town is built by code (`building()`, `stall()` and so on), with no world data to edit. If every placed thing is an operation (add a stall at x, z; remove a palm; move a board), then: *players building* is those operations saved on the phone; a *shared live world* is the same operations sent through a server; a *live editor* is those operations applied instantly plus an export; *streaming* is loading the operations for the region around you. The plan is to add this as a **dynamic layer on top of the hand-built town** (the hand-built buildings stay in code and are not editable; placed things are data), with collision and the town audit aware of it, and a phone-friendly Build mode (tap to place from a small set: stall, bench, palm, lamp, board). Order: (1) the web build above (done); (2) the change list, its save, and Build mode, offline; (3) Mini Militia offline; (4) the server, for the shared world and online matches; (5) streaming last, because the area-by-area design (bus between districts) already handles size on phones.

**Mini Militia style mode: offline against bots first (decided).** Side-view arenas on an orthographic camera using our robot and human characters; **landscape phone controls like the original: left thumb walks and thrusts the jetpack, right thumb aims and fires**; weapons (pistol, SMG, shotgun, sniper, rocket, grenade, melee), jetpack fuel, health and ammo pickups, respawn, bots to fill the seats. The rules go in **one pure module with Node tests** (like the town and ride rules) so that the same file later runs on the server. Arenas in the real places: Kozhikode Beach, the Churam bends, the Zil Money roof, the Manjeri turf; bought costumes show in the match, coins are the payout. **Online later:** a small Node WebSocket server that decides hits (about 20 snapshots a second, the phone predicts its own movement and smooths the others), room codes for friends, bots filling empty seats; it needs hosting. Peer-to-peer (one phone as host) needs no game server but is easy to cheat in and still needs a small matchmaking server. Online netcode is the hard part, which is why it comes second.

## 8g. DEAD SIGNAL: the main story (decided 2026-10-27; replaces "The Last Mile")

**The pitch (from the user's brief).** A psychological techno-thriller across Malappuram, Kozhikode and Wayanad.
